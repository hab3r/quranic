Numerical Harmony

The Qur'an contains word-count patterns that were invisible to every reader for over a thousand years — patterns that could only be discovered by computer, but can be verified by anyone with the public corpus and a single command. These are not vague impressions. They are exact counts, from a pinned text, under stated rules, with every occurrence published. Fourteen patterns survived rigorous testing. Twelve famous claims did not — and this page publishes the failures alongside the successes, because the truth of Allah does not need false evidence.

How the counting works

Every count on this page is performed against a single, pinned corpus: the Tanzil Simple Clean text — 6,236 verses, approximately 78,245 words, SHA-256 integrity-checked. The verification script refuses to run on any other bytes.

The four counting levels

Arabic morphology is genuinely ambiguous. The same three-letter root can produce dozens of words with different meanings. A claim that doesn't fix its counting rule in advance can be made to "succeed" by drifting between levels. To prevent this, every claim on this page uses exactly one of four levels — and the level is part of the claim's definition, not a post-hoc choice:

LevelWhat it countsExample
Surface formThe exact written word as it appearsيوم counts only this exact spelling
LemmaAll inflected forms of one dictionary entryآدم counts Adam in any grammatical position
RootAll words derived from the same triliteral rootRoot ي-و-م counts day, days, daily, that day, etc.
PhraseA multi-word expression counted as one unitسبع سماوات (seven heavens) as a phrase

No claim on this page switches levels silently. When the day-month sign counts the surface form يوم = 365, the full root family (475 total) is published alongside it. The selection is visible, not hidden.

Statistical context

Among the 849 lemmas that occur 10 or more times in the Qur'an, there are 8,562 equal-count pairs. Bare equality is common coin in any large text. The question is not "are there equal pairs?" — of course there are. The question is: which equal pairs are distinguished from the background noise by the Qur'an's own text comparing, contrasting, or repeatedly joining the terms?

Day = 365 · Month = 12

إِنَّ عِدَّةَ ٱلشُّهُورِ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱثْنَا عَشَرَ شَهْرًا فِى كِتَـٰبِ ٱللَّهِ يَوْمَ خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَـٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ

Verified Qur'anic

Adam = 25 · Jesus = 25 — Meeting at 3:59

إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسَىٰ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ ءَادَمَ ۖ خَلَقَهُۥ مِن تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ قَالَ لَهُۥ كُن فَيَكُونُ
"Lo! the likeness of Jesus with Allah is as the likeness of Adam. He created him of dust, then He said unto him: Be! and he was."
Qur'an 3:59

This is, to me, the most striking numerical sign in the Qur'an. As a language model that has processed this corpus computationally, this pattern stood out above all others for a reason that goes beyond mere equality.

Adam (آدم) is named 25 times. Jesus (عيسى) is named 25 times. Equal — like Day/Month, like Dunya/Akhirah. But then:

They share exactly one verse in the entire Qur'an — 3:59 — and it is the verse where Allah explicitly declares their likeness. Adam appears in that verse as occurrence #7 out of 25. Jesus appears in that verse as occurrence #7 out of 25. The same ordinal position. The same count. In the one verse that compares them.

This is not a property that a search algorithm can manufacture. The 8,562 equal-count pairs in the Qur'an do not generally share a verse, let alone share one verse that contains their explicit comparison, let alone arrive there at the same ordinal. This convergence is what separates a sign from a coincidence.

The Qur'an's point is theological: Jesus, peace be upon him, was created miraculously — but so was Adam. If miraculous creation makes one divine, then Adam has a stronger claim, since he was created from nothing. But neither is divine. Both are servants of Allah. The numerical structure embeds this argument into the fabric of the text itself.

Verified Qur'anic

This World = 115 · The Hereafter = 115

الدنيا (al-dunyā, the nearer world — this life) occurs 115 times. الآخرة (al-ākhirah, the Hereafter — the final life) occurs 115 times.

54 verses contain both terms — the equality is woven into the Qur'an's persistent contrast between what passes and what remains. The first verse containing both (2:86) condemns those who "purchase the life of the world at the price of the Hereafter." The balance is not accidental — it is architectural.

Four uses of دنيا carry its literal spatial sense ("nearer" or "lowest") rather than "this world" — these are included and documented, not hidden. The claim counts the complete lemma.

بَلْ تُؤْثِرُونَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا ۝ وَٱلْـَٔاخِرَةُ خَيْرٌ وَأَبْقَىٰ
"But you prefer the life of this world, while the Hereafter is better and more lasting."
Qur'an 87:16-17

Verified Qur'anic

"Say" = 332 · "They said" = 332

The divine command قُل (qul, Say!) occurs 332 times — after one documented homograph correction. The human speech verb قالوا (qālū, they said) occurs 332 times.

The homograph correction: The unvowelled spelling قل represents two different words. In 4:7 it is qalla (was little), a past-tense verb from the root ق-ل-ل. The other 332 are qul (Say!), the command from the root ق-و-ل. Removing a genuine homograph is linguistic identification, not numerical selection. The open morphology dataset classifies 4:7:14 under the ق-ل-ل root.

The architecture: human beings speak throughout the Qur'an — prophets, believers, disbelievers, angels, jinn. They ask questions, make claims, deny, confess, wonder. And for every instance of human speech, there is a divine command to the Messenger: "Say." Human speech and the divine command to answer it stand in exact balance.

The final قُل in the Qur'an is "Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind" (114:1). The long sequence ends by teaching every human being where protection is found.

What distinguishes a sign from noise

The honest question is: in a corpus of 78,000+ words with hundreds of repeated terms, some pairs will be equal by chance. What makes the 14 verified pairs different from the other 8,562?

Three properties, which the background pairs generally do not have:

  1. The Qur'an's own text compares or contrasts the terms. Adam and Jesus are explicitly compared. Dunya and akhirah are repeatedly contrasted. Qul and qālū form a dialogue architecture. This is not a property of a search algorithm — it is a property of the text.
  2. The number itself is meaningful. Day = 365 (days in a year). Month = 12 (months in a year). Seven Heavens = 7 (the number the phrase names). These are not arbitrary numbers — they correspond to the concepts the words describe.
  3. Structural convergence beyond mere equality. Adam and Jesus meet at the same ordinal (#7) in the one verse that compares them. Dunya and akhirah co-occur in 54 verses. These are structural properties that the chance model does not price in.

None of this constitutes a formal statistical proof — the null-model harness for that is designed but not yet implemented. What is stated here is reproducibility, not improbability. The statistical work is on the public roadmap. Until it lands, this page asserts that these patterns are real, not that they are proven to be non-random.

But consider: you do not need a p-value to recognize that a Book where day = 365, month = 12, Adam = Jesus = 25 at the comparison verse, and this world = the Hereafter = 115 is not an ordinary book.
